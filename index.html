An ordinary Tuesday.

Four quotes, four timelines.

What you get

Four systems that feed each other.

Holders stake to earn coins. Coins pay to play, playing wins gems, and gems buy shop items and raffle tickets. Each system gives the next one a reason to exist, so holders keep moving through your site instead of visiting once.

01 / 04

NFT staking

Staking that keeps NFTs in the wallet.

  • Holders stake from their own wallet and the NFT never moves.
  • You set the daily reward, a cap per wallet and how many days rewards can pile up.
  • A welcome gift on a first stake lets a new holder play straight away.

Gaming

A game worth a few minutes a day.

  • Echo Vault is a memory card game with ten levels, bosses, seasons and achievements.
  • Coins pay the entry fee, so your staking reward decides how much a holder can play.
  • The board stays on the server. Rounds that look like a bot have their prize held for your review.

Marketplace

Somewhere to spend what players win.

  • Gems buy power ups, boosts, card backs, backgrounds and flair.
  • You set the items, prices, stock and limits, and caps keep stacked boosts in check.
  • Coins and gems are not tokens and cannot be cashed out, so there is nothing to list, pump or dump.

Raffles

Raffles people can check for themselves.

  • Tickets cost coins or gems. Raffles open, close and draw on their own.
  • The draw is published as a fingerprint first and revealed after, so anyone can verify the winner in their browser.
  • Prizes can be NFTs, gems, shop items or roles, and winners are told in the site's notification bell.

The control room

You run it without a developer on call.

  • A balance simulator that plays the game before you change a fee or a prize.
  • An economy page showing the coins and gems created and spent each day.
  • A ledger check that proves in one click that every balance matches its history.
  • Analytics that show which part of your site holders spend their time on.

FAQ

“Can you build us a custom game?”

The $1,648 is an installation fee, not a development fee. It covers branding and configuration of what exists, and what exists already includes Echo Vault, a finished memory game with ten levels, seasons and achievements. A new game is a different project, quoted separately.

“Why not build the four systems ourselves?”

You can, and you would own every line. But you would be building staking, a game, a marketplace and raffles at once, plus the ledger, security and admin that hold them together. The real comparison is your calendar, not a price.

Ethereum and Base are supported, plus compatible EVM networks. EVM compatible doesn't guarantee that every contract behaves the same way, so I check your network and contract standard before an installation is accepted.

You keep your brand, your logos and your materials. The platform's source code stays with its developer, and the installation fee is not a code sale. What you buy is a working, branded installation with hosting and support.

Proof: it is written in section 9 of the installation and service terms.

“What happens after the 12 months?”

Hosting for the first 12 months is included, with no monthly charge. After that, hosting continues under a separate renewal agreement.

Proof: ask for the renewal terms at the demo, before you decide.

“Is it safe for our holders?”

Holders sign in by signing a message. The site never asks for a transaction or an approval and never holds a private key. Staking is a record on your site, so NFTs stay in the holder's wallet, and coins and gems are not tokens, so there is nothing to drain. It has not had an independent security audit, and I'll say so on the call.

Proof: the platform ships with an automated security scan that you can run against your own site.

“We already have staking. Why add this?”

Staking alone gives holders a reason to click once a day, but the coins have nowhere to go. A game, a marketplace and raffles give them somewhere, which is what turns a daily click into a habit.

Proof: 14 of 17 Secrets of the World players talk about raffles or winning NFT prizes, and 11 talk about the variety of things to do.

“Are raffles legal for us?”

Rules for raffles, prizes and paid entry differ by country, and this page is not legal advice. The platform gives you the mechanism, including tickets paid in coins that holders earn by staking. Check the rules where you operate before you run one.

Proof: every draw is public and verifiable, which also helps you show it was fair.
